## Ingredients

Makes 4 servings, per the source.

### For the koftas
- 50 g boiled & squeezed soya chunks (blended)
- 100 g high-protein low-fat paneer
- 1 small onion, finely chopped
- 2–3 green chillies, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
- 1 tbsp coriander powder
- 1 tsp red chilli powder
- Pinch of garam masala
- Salt to taste
- 1 tbsp besan

### For the gravy
- 1 tsp ghee
- 2 onions, thinly sliced
- 2 green cardamoms
- 1-inch cinnamon stick
- 4–5 cloves
- 7–8 garlic cloves
- 4–5 green chillies
- 8–10 cashews
- Handful of fresh coriander
- 1 tsp ghee
- 2 tsp butter
- 1 tbsp coriander powder
- 2 tsp cumin powder
- 1 tbsp chaat masala
- 1/2 tbsp kasuri methi
- 1 tsp garam masala
- Salt to taste
- Pinch of sugar
- 1 small cup room-temperature curd
- Water, as required

### To finish
- Low-fat cream — amount not specified
- Chilli oil — amount not specified
- Fresh coriander

## Instructions

1. Caramelize the sliced onions, garlic, green chillies, cashews and whole spices in
   1 tsp ghee.
2. Mix all the kofta ingredients, shape into koftas and pan-cook until golden.
3. Remove the cinnamon stick and blend the caramelized mixture with the coriander and
   a little water.
4. Heat the ghee and butter, roast the spices, then add the blended paste and cook
   for 4–5 minutes.
5. Add water, salt and a pinch of sugar. Lower the flame and stir in the
   room-temperature curd, stirring continuously.
6. Add the koftas, simmer for 2–3 minutes and finish with low-fat cream, chilli oil
   and fresh coriander.

## Notes

Claude: rung 1 — the full recipe was in the post caption, read through the browser.
The steps are the creator's, lightly tidied.

Claude: the cream and chilli oil in step 6 are not in the source's ingredient list —
they only appear in the method, with no amounts. I've listed them under "To finish"
so they don't get missed when shopping.

Claude: "50 g boiled & squeezed soya chunks" reads as the weight *after* boiling and
squeezing, which is only about 20 g dry — a small handful. If it means 50 g dry, the
kofta mix roughly doubles in bulk and the 1 tbsp besan won't bind it. Start from
about 20 g dry, boil, squeeze hard and weigh; check against the video the first time.

Claude: the source says soya chunks, but they're blended before they go into the mix,
so soaked and squeezed granules do the same job and skip the blending. Filed under
`soya granules`, the one soya term this collection uses.

Claude: squeeze the soya properly before blending. Wet soya is the usual reason
koftas fall apart in the pan, and besan can only absorb so much.

Claude: "high-protein low-fat paneer" is a specific product. Ordinary paneer works
and binds better — it's richer, and a little lower in protein for the same weight.

Claude: the two lots of ghee are separate uses — the first 1 tsp is for caramelizing
at step 1, the second, with the butter, is for roasting the ground spices at step 4.
"The spices" at step 4 are the coriander powder, cumin powder, chaat masala, kasuri
methi and garam masala.

Claude: the source removes only the cinnamon before blending, so the cardamom and
cloves go into the paste. That's a lot of clove for one gravy — use 4, not 5, if
you're unsure.

Claude: the curd will split if it goes in cold or over high heat. The source's three
precautions at step 5 — room temperature, flame lowered, stirring continuously — all
matter. Whisk it smooth first, and don't let the gravy boil hard afterwards.

Claude: the source gives 18 g protein per serving at 4 servings, which sits just
under the 20 g bar, so this isn't tagged `high-protein`. That serving is small —
about 235 kcal by the source's figure. **For the two of you, cook the whole recipe
and split it**: that's a proper main with roti or rice, and at that portion it does
clear the bar.

Claude: nothing here needs an oven. The koftas could go in the air fryer instead of
the pan — about 180°C for 10–12 minutes, turned once, brushed with a little oil —
which is gentler on a soft mix than flipping in a pan. That's my suggestion, not the
source's.

Claude: this is the second soya-and-besan recipe here, after the
[soya seekh kebab](soya-seekh-kebab.md). That one uses granules and stays dry; this
uses blended chunks and goes into a gravy. The kebab mix would work shaped as koftas
in this gravy if you have it made.
