## Ingredients

### Soup
- Oil of your choice
- 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 4–5 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1 small or medium potato, diced small
- Water, to just cover
- 1 bay leaf
- 200–250 g shelled green peas
- 100 g baby spinach
- Fresh mint leaves
- Zest of 1 lemon
- Lemon juice, to taste
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- Extra virgin olive oil, to finish

### Pistachio sesame dukkah
- About 3 tbsp pistachios
- About 1 tbsp white sesame seeds
- 1/2 tsp lemon zest
- Pinch of salt
- Black pepper (optional)

## Instructions

1. Warm the oil in a heavy pot. Add the cumin seeds until they crackle. Add the
   onion with a pinch of salt and cook until soft and sweet.
2. Add the garlic and cook briefly.
3. Add the potato and enough water to just cover. Add the bay leaf. Simmer gently
   until the potato is tender.
4. Add the peas and cook 2–3 minutes. Stir in the spinach to wilt for 30–60
   seconds. Turn off the heat.
5. Strain, reserving the cooking liquid. Cool slightly, then add the mint leaves
   and lemon zest and blend right away until silky and bright.
6. Loosen with the reserved hot liquid to a smooth pour. Season with salt, black
   pepper and lemon juice.
7. For the dukkah, toast the pistachios and sesame on low heat until fragrant.
   Cool slightly, then crush to a coarse mix with the lemon zest and a pinch of
   salt. Add black pepper if you like.
8. Rewarm the soup gently. Bowl it, drizzle with extra virgin olive oil, spoon
   over the dukkah, and finish with a few mint leaves.

## Source notes

From the creator, in her own words:

- Blend right after the mint and zest go in, to keep the bright green.
- Reheat gently and avoid boiling.
- She forgot the bay leaf while filming — it should be added at step 3 for gentle
  aroma.

## Notes

Claude: the two source notes above are the whole technique. Mint browns within
minutes of heat and the soup goes from bright green to olive, so have the blender
ready before step 4 finishes.

Claude: straining and reserving the liquid at step 5 rather than blending
everything together is what gives you control over thickness. Don't tip it all in.

Claude: no serving count is stated. I've assumed 3 from 250 g peas and a potato.
For your two, it's a generous bowl each with a little left.

Claude: make the dukkah first if you're cooking alone. It's the one component that
can be done ahead and it keeps for weeks in a jar — worth tripling.

Claude: around 8 g protein per serving. Light and fresh rather than filling,
despite the peas.
