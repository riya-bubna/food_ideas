## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp unsalted butter (28 g)
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
- 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 cup vegetable broth (236 ml)
- 4 oz cream cheese (113 g)
- 2 × 15-oz cans butter beans (425 g each), drained
- 1/2 cup shredded parmesan (40 g)

## Instructions

1. **Flavour base.** Melt the butter over medium heat in a large sauté pan or medium
   pot. Add the garlic, Italian seasoning, black pepper and smoked paprika. Cook until
   the garlic is soft and fragrant, 1–2 minutes.
2. **Simmer.** Stir in the vegetable broth and cream cheese, stirring until the cream
   cheese has fully melted. Add the drained butter beans and simmer uncovered for
   10–15 minutes until the sauce thickens. Stir gently to keep the beans intact.
3. **Finish.** Stir in the parmesan and serve warm.

- **Storage:** airtight in the fridge 3–4 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding

## Notes

Claude: two 425 g cans drained is roughly **480–500 g of cooked beans, or about
260–300 g dry** — which matches the creator's own 3–3 1/2 cups. Overnight soak plus a
pressure cook, so this is a planned meal for you rather than the 20-minute weeknight
dish the timing suggests.

Claude: **making it without the cream cheese.** The simplest version adds nothing in
its place: simmer as written, then mash about a third of the beans against the side of
the pan and stir them back. Butter beans are starchy enough to make their own sauce.
Two things become necessary once the cream cheese is gone, though — **1–2 tsp lemon
juice**, because cream cheese was cultured and its tang is what the dish tastes flat
without, and **a little more fat**, either a third tablespoon of butter or a glug of
olive oil, since you have dropped roughly 38 g of it. Keep the full 40 g of parmesan;
it is carrying more salt, fat and umami here than its volume suggests.

Claude: for a richer no-cream-cheese version, **40 g cashews soaked in hot water 15–20
minutes and blended with the cup of broth** gets closest to the original — the same
move as the queso in the [black bean quesadillas](black-bean-quesadillas.md).
**Hung curd** is the closest on flavour, being properly tangy, but whisk it with a few
spoons of the hot sauce to temper it and stir it in **off the heat** — it will split if
it boils.

Claude: **nutritional yeast instead of parmesan changes the method, not just the
flavour.** Parmesan is what emulsifies this sauce — melting proteins plus roughly 30%
fat. Nutritional yeast is a flavour powder: savoury, but non-melting, almost fat-free
and low in sodium, and it *absorbs* liquid rather than binding it, so too much turns
the sauce powdery. Going that route, the body has to come from elsewhere: **mash half
the beans rather than a third**, take the butter up to 3 tbsp (or butter plus olive
oil), stir in **2–3 tbsp of yeast off the heat**, and salt the dish directly. A squeeze
of lemon goes back in — yeast flakes are savoury but flat, with none of parmesan's
sharpness — and 1 tsp of miso covers the aged funk they also lack. For genuine
creaminess, blend 30 g cashews into the bean liquid: that is exactly why the queso in
the [black bean quesadillas](black-bean-quesadillas.md) pairs yeast *with* cashews
rather than using it alone.

Claude: about 13 g protein per serving as written, so this is a side or a light main
rather than dinner on its own. With bread it's a meal; halved for two it's a generous
side next to something with more protein.

Claude: no oven, one pan, and the only equipment beyond that is a blender if you take
the tofu route.

Claude: butter beans are new to this collection — nothing else here uses them. Closest
in spirit is the creamy-bean-and-cheese register of the
[black bean quesadillas](black-bean-quesadillas.md).
