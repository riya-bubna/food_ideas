**Source:** My own — no external recipe.

> **This is a template.** I make it two ways: glass noodles with mango and
> paneer, or shirataki noodles with edamame and/or grilled tofu. Both in soy sauce.

## Ingredients

Cooked by eye; no quantities recorded.

### Base
- Glass noodles
- Soy sauce
- Sesame seeds

### The version photographed
- Mango, sliced into strips
- Paneer, cubed and grilled with turmeric
- Red cabbage, shredded
- Carrot, julienned
- Green beans

### The other version
- Shirataki noodles instead of glass noodles
- Edamame and/or grilled tofu instead of paneer

## Instructions

1. Soak or boil the glass noodles per the packet, then drain and rinse in cold water
   so they stop cooking.
2. Grill the paneer with turmeric until golden at the edges.
3. Shred the red cabbage, julienne the carrot, and slice the beans and mango into
   strips.
4. Toss the noodles with the vegetables, mango and soy sauce.
5. Add the grilled paneer and scatter over sesame seeds.

## Notes

Me: my own, and made in two forms — glass noodles with mango and paneer, or
shirataki with edamame and/or grilled tofu.

Claude: the two versions aren't interchangeable nutritionally, which is worth knowing
when picking. Glass noodles are mung bean starch and this version tags `carb-heavy`.
Shirataki is almost pure fibre and water — that version would be `low-carb` and, with
edamame plus tofu, likely `high-protein` too. If you want the leaner one, it's the
shirataki build, not this one.

Claude: rinse the noodles cold at step 1. Glass noodles keep cooking in residual heat
and turn to a single gummy mass otherwise — the most common way this dish fails.

Claude: shirataki needs a hard rinse and a dry pan-fry before it goes in. Straight
from the packet it carries a smell that soy sauce won't cover.

Claude: the mango goes in raw and last. It's doing the same job as in your
[mango paneer stir fry](mango-paneer-stir-fry.md) — sweetness and bite against the
salt — and it collapses if it's tossed while anything is still hot.

Claude: no quantities recorded, since you build it by eye. Worth adding rough amounts
if your cook is ever making it.
