## Ingredients

Serves 8 as a side, without the toppings.

### Salad
- 1 large red cabbage (about 2–2.5 lb / 900 g–1.1 kg), thinly sliced
- 2–3 tbsp olive oil (30–45 ml)
- 1/2 large cooked beet (about 3 oz / 85 g), chopped
- 1 cup creamy beet dressing (260 g — half of what the dressing recipe below makes)
- Salt, to taste
- Black pepper, to taste

### Beet dressing
- 3/4 cup cashews (100 g), soaked in hot water for 15 minutes
- 2–3 cloves (can be roasted in foil alongside the cabbage if you prefer them cooked)
- 1 lemon, juiced and zested
- 1/2 beetroot, cooked
- 2 chilli peppers (optional)
- 2 tbsp nutritional yeast
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1/4 cup water (add more as needed)
- Salt and pepper, to taste

### Optional, for serving
- Crispy tofu
- Toasted walnuts, almonds, or pumpkin seeds
- Toasted sourdough
- Fresh herbs
- Chilli flakes

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F / 220°C.
2. Thinly slice the cabbage using a mandolin or sharp knife.
3. Spread the cabbage over one large baking sheet, or divide between two trays if
   necessary to prevent crowding.
4. Drizzle with the olive oil and season with salt and black pepper. Toss well, then
   spread the cabbage into an even layer.
5. Roast for 30–40 minutes, tossing halfway through, until softened, with browned and
   slightly crispy edges.
6. Transfer the roasted cabbage to a large bowl and add the grated cooked beet.
7. Pour over about 1 cup (260 g) of the creamy beet dressing and toss thoroughly
   until all of the cabbage and beet is well coated.
8. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper.
9. Serve warm or cold, with crispy tofu, toasted nuts and sourdough if desired.

## Source notes

From the creator:

- Green cabbage can be used instead of red.
- If the cabbage is sliced very thinly, begin checking it after 15 minutes.
- Avoid overcrowding the tray if you want browned edges.
- For meal prep, store the roasted cabbage and dressing separately and combine before
  serving. It softens once dressed but is still good cold the next day.
- Add tofu, chickpeas, lentils or tempeh to turn it into a higher-protein meal.
- Keeps 3–4 days in the fridge in an airtight container.

Claude: **the source gives no method for the dressing**, only its ingredients. Drain
the soaked cashews and blend everything until completely smooth, adding water a
little at a time until it pours. That step is mine.

Claude: the "2–3 cloves" line in the dressing doesn't name what they're cloves of.
It's garlic — the note about roasting them in foil only makes sense for garlic — but
the word isn't in the source.

Claude: the salad list says the beet is *chopped*; step 6 says *grated*. Grated
spreads the colour through the cabbage better.

Claude: one beet covers the whole recipe — half in the salad, half in the dressing.
It needs to be cooked first: a beet steams fine in the microwave, about 8–10 minutes
in a covered bowl with a splash of water.

Claude: the dressing recipe makes about 2 cups and the salad uses one. The other half
keeps a few days in the fridge and works as a dip.

Claude: **this is an oven recipe and you don't have one.** Air fryer: 205°C, in a
shallow layer, shaking every 5 minutes, and start checking at 15–20 minutes — sliced
cabbage is light, moves in the airflow and chars fast. A full 1 kg cabbage would be
three or four batches, which isn't worth it. **Make a quarter of the recipe for the
two of you** — about 250 g cabbage, 2 tsp oil — and it's one batch, maybe two.

Claude: a quarter recipe as a side for two: 250 g red cabbage, 2 tsp olive oil, about
20 g grated cooked beet, 1/4 cup (65 g) dressing. The dressing is awkward to blend in
a quantity that small, so make half of it — 50 g cashews, 1 garlic clove, 1/2 lemon,
1/4 beet, 1 chilli, 1 tbsp nutritional yeast, 1/2 tbsp Dijon — and keep the rest.

Claude: if the air fryer is busy, a wide kadhai or pan on high heat does the same
job — cabbage in a thin layer, left alone long enough to catch before stirring. You
get char without the crisp edges.

Claude: about 4 g protein a serving by the source's own figure. This is a side until
something goes on top. The source says crispy tofu; grilled paneer does the same job,
and blending 50 g of paneer into the dressing would lift it further.
