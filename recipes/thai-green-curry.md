## Ingredients

### Green paste
- 2 cups fresh coriander
- 15 shallots
- 4 green chillies
- 4 inch lemongrass stalk
- 6 garlic cloves
- 2 tbsp galangal
- 1 tsp coriander seeds
- 1/2 tsp peppercorns
- 1 tsp cumin
- 1 tbsp lemon zest (zest of 1 whole lemon)
- 1/4 tsp turmeric powder

### Curry
- 2 tbsp oil
- 350 ml water
- 3 kaffir lime leaves
- 2 cups mixed vegetables
- 400 ml thick coconut milk
- 1 tbsp jaggery or brown sugar
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- Basil leaves
- Salt

## Instructions

1. Grind all the green paste ingredients together into a smooth paste.
2. Heat the oil and sauté the paste for 2 minutes.
3. Add 350 ml water, the kaffir lime leaves, salt and the mixed vegetables.
4. Cook until done but still crunchy — do not let the vegetables go soft.
5. Lower the flame. Add the coconut milk, jaggery, soy sauce and basil leaves.
6. Let it come to a first boil, then switch off the heat immediately.
7. Serve steaming hot with fragrant rice.

## Notes

Claude: the creator's own vegetable mix was yellow and green zucchini, broccoli,
tofu and baby corn. The recipe just says "2 cups mixed vegetables", so treat that
as the reference combination.

Claude: the method adds kaffir lime leaves twice — at step 3 and again at step 5 —
but the ingredient list gives only 3 total. Split them, or add all three at step 3.
Probably a slip in the caption.

Claude: step 6 matters. Coconut milk splits into oil and solids if it boils for
more than a moment, so kill the heat at the first bubble.

Claude: adding tofu to the vegetable mix is what makes this a meal rather than a
side. Without it there's very little protein here despite the volume.

Claude: the green paste freezes well in portions. It's the only laborious part, so
making a triple batch turns this into a 15-minute weeknight curry thereafter.

Claude: galangal is not interchangeable with ginger — it's sharper and more
citrussy. If you can only get ginger, the curry works but stops tasting Thai.
