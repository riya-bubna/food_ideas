**Source:** [Fried Rice n chilli-garlic paneer gravy, Bristi Home Kitchen](https://youtu.be/HWtD8_NaFJk)

Only the fried rice is recorded here — the video also makes a chilli-garlic paneer
gravy to go with it.

## Ingredients

The source lists ingredients without quantities for the vegetables and rice.

- 1 tsp oil
- 1 tsp garlic, chopped
- 1 tsp ginger, chopped
- Spring onion, chopped
- Bell pepper, chopped
- Carrot, chopped
- 1 tsp green chilli, chopped
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1 tsp vinegar
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp chilli sauce
- Boiled rice
- Chopped spring onion, to finish

## Instructions

1. Heat the oil over high heat. Add the chopped garlic, ginger and green chilli and
   cook briefly, until fragrant.
2. Add the white parts of the spring onion, the bell pepper and the carrot. Stir-fry
   on high so they take colour but stay crunchy.
3. Season with the salt, vinegar, soy sauce and chilli sauce.
4. Add the boiled rice and toss until every grain is coated and heated through.
5. Finish with the chopped spring onion greens.

## Notes

Claude: the ingredient list is complete but the video gives no vegetable or rice
quantities. Step wording is mine — the description lists ingredients in cooking order
rather than writing out a method.

Claude: use **cold, day-old rice**. Freshly boiled rice steams and clumps in the pan.
This is the single thing that separates fried rice from a rice stir-fry, and the
source doesn't say it.

Claude: highest heat you have, and don't crowd the pan. If you're doubling this, cook
in two batches.

Claude: this is your first rice dish that stands as a main rather than sitting under a
bowl — it fills a real gap.

Claude: almost no protein on its own. The source pairs it with a chilli-garlic paneer
gravy, and the obvious partner already in your collection is the
[air-fryer paneer tikka](air-fryer-paneer-tikka.md), which is the same Indo-Chinese
register without the deep-frying. The
[basil tofu stir fry](basil-tofu-stir-fry.md) would work too.

Claude: 1 tsp each of vinegar, soy and chilli sauce is restrained. Taste at step 3 —
the seasoning has to carry the whole dish, and it scales with how much rice you use.
