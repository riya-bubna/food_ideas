## Ingredients

- 1 medium cauliflower, cut into florets
- 2–3 garlic cloves
- 1 medium onion, sliced
- 2 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- Salt to taste
- 2 cups water
- 1/2 cup milk
- Fresh chives, to garnish
- Toasted bread, to serve

## Instructions

1. Heat the butter and olive oil in a pan over medium heat.
2. Add the garlic and sliced onion and sauté. Then add the cauliflower, black
   pepper and salt.
3. Sauté until the cauliflower begins to soften and the onions turn translucent.
   The cauliflower should be nicely golden.
4. Pour in the 2 cups of water, cover, and simmer until the cauliflower is tender.
5. Transfer to a blender and blend until smooth.
6. Warm the remaining butter in the same pan. Pour the blended soup back in and
   stir to a smooth, creamy consistency.
7. Adjust the seasoning, warm gently, and stir in the milk.
8. Serve topped with charred cauliflower, parmesan and fresh chives, with a drizzle
   of olive oil.

## Notes

Claude: two gaps in the source. Step 6 calls for "the remaining tablespoon of
butter" but all 2 tbsp went in at step 1 — hold a tablespoon back if you want to
follow it. And step 8 garnishes with charred cauliflower and parmesan, neither of
which appears in the ingredient list. Reserve a handful of florets to char
separately if you want the garnish as shown.

Claude: the source states no serving count — a commenter asked and got no reply.
I've assumed 4 from one medium cauliflower and 2.5 cups of liquid. For your two,
halve it or keep the rest; it reheats well.

Claude: step 3 is where the flavour is. Take the cauliflower properly golden
rather than just soft — a pale sauté gives you a thin, sweet soup instead of a
nutty one.

Claude: add the milk off the boil at step 7. It splits if the soup is bubbling
when it goes in.

Claude: about 4 g protein per serving. This is a light starter or a lunch with
bread, not a main on its own.
