The creator describes it as her take on mango sticky rice, from a trip to Thailand.

## Ingredients

Only the dates carry a stated quantity — everything else is by eye.

### Pancake batter
- Oats
- Desiccated coconut
- 2–3 dates
- Vanilla essence
- Warm milk, enough to soak
- Baking powder

### Coconut sauce
- Coconut milk (fresh is better; canned works)
- Salt
- Honey
- Corn flour

### To serve
- Mango slices
- Honey
- Kalonji (nigella seeds)

## Instructions

1. Put the oats, desiccated coconut, dates and vanilla essence in a bowl. Pour over
   warm milk and leave to soak for 10 minutes.
2. Meanwhile, put the coconut milk in a pan with salt, honey and corn flour. Cook on
   a low flame, stirring continuously, until it thickens to a pourable slurry. Set
   aside to cool.
3. Blend the soaked oat mixture until smooth.
4. Stir in the baking powder and mix well.
5. Cook as small pancakes on a pan.
6. To serve, put a pancake on a plate, spoon over the thickened coconut milk, and
   pile on plenty of mango slices.
7. Drizzle with honey and sprinkle with kalonji.

## Notes

Claude: recovered by transcribing the Hindi narration. Neither the Instagram caption
nor the YouTube description contains a recipe, and the video has no on-screen text
beyond one aside from the creator. The ingredient identities and the method are hers;
the step wording is mine.

Claude: no quantities are given for anything except the dates, so this is a
cook-by-feel recipe. As a starting point for two: about 1 cup oats, 1/4 cup
desiccated coconut, and enough warm milk to just cover. Treat that as my suggestion.

Claude: the dates are the only sweetener in the batter — there's no sugar. They need
the full 10-minute soak to soften enough to blend smooth.

Claude: stir the coconut sauce continuously at step 2. Corn flour catches on the pan
base the moment you stop, and you can't rescue a scorched coconut sauce.

Claude: baking powder goes in *after* blending, at step 4. Blended in, it loses lift
before the batter reaches the pan.

Claude: kalonji against mango and coconut is the unusual note here and it isn't a
garnish you can skip — it's what stops the dish being purely sweet.
