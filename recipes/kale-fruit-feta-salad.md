**Source:** My own version, inspired by
[@kalefornia_kravings](https://www.instagram.com/p/DaVixgLPpEj/) — a peach and
blueberry salad over mixed greens with candied pecans, olive oil and balsamic glaze.
I swapped the greens for kale and the fruit for strawberry, black grapes and
orange.

> **This is a template, not a fixed recipe.** I swap the fruit for whatever's
> around — cherries and red grapes as well as the strawberries, black grapes and
> orange in the photographed version.

## Ingredients

Cooked by eye; no quantities recorded.

- Kale, torn into bite-size pieces
- Strawberries, sliced
- Black grapes
- Orange, segmented
- Feta, crumbled
- Balsamic dressing

Other fruit that works: cherries, red grapes.

## Instructions

1. Tear the kale off its stems into bite-size pieces.
2. Slice the strawberries, halve the grapes if large, and segment the orange.
3. Scatter the fruit over the kale.
4. Crumble the feta over the top.
5. Drizzle with balsamic dressing and serve.

## Notes

Me: my own recipe, and a deliberately flexible one — the fruit changes with
what's in the house.

Claude: raw kale is tough, and massaging it is what fixes that. Rub the torn leaves
with a little olive oil and a pinch of salt for a minute until they darken and
soften before anything else goes in. It's the single biggest improvement available
to a raw kale salad.

Claude: dress it at the table, not ahead. Balsamic collapses the strawberries and
draws water out of the orange within about ten minutes.

Claude: the citrus and the feta are doing opposite jobs — sweet-acidic against
salt. If you swap the orange out for cherries or grapes alone, the salad loses its
acidity, so add a squeeze of lemon or more balsamic to compensate.

Claude: kale holds up far better than the mixed greens the original uses. Worth
keeping this one for when you want a salad that won't wilt on the table.

Claude: the one element of the original you've dropped is candied pecans. They add
the crunch that kale, soft fruit and feta otherwise lack — worth trying if the salad
ever feels one-note in texture.

Claude: quantities aren't recorded because you build it by eye. Worth noting rough
amounts if your cook is ever making it.
