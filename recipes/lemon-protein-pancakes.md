**Source:** [@madelainerascan on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DbbhjPWjtPy/)

> **Vegetarian.** The eggs here are structural, not a garnish — see the notes
> for what changes. This one needs reformulating, not just omitting.

## Ingredients

Makes 1 serving.

### Pancakes
- 40 g rolled oats
- 150 g 1% cottage cheese
- 1 whole egg
- 40 g egg whites
- 1 1/2 tsp baking powder
- Zest of 1/2 to 1 lemon (she used about 3/4, saving the rest for the topping)
- Juice from a lemon wedge, or more
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Sweetener of choice (she used monkfruit)
- Pinch of salt

### Topping
- 125 g 0% Greek yogurt
- Zest of about 1/2 lemon
- A splash of vanilla
- Sweetener to taste

## Instructions

1. Blend the oats to a fine flour.
2. Add the cottage cheese, egg, egg whites, vanilla, lemon zest, lemon juice,
   sweetener and salt. Blend until smooth.
3. Add the baking powder and pulse briefly — don't over-blend, or it pressurises and
   erupts when you open the lid.
4. Rest the batter 5+ minutes if you can; it thickens.
5. Cook in a lightly greased pan over medium-low heat **with the lid on**. Once the
   tops are mostly set, flip and cook until golden.
6. Mix the Greek yogurt with the remaining zest, vanilla and sweetener, and spoon over
   the pancakes.

## Notes

Claude: those macros include the egg and egg whites, which supply roughly 10 g of that
46 g. Removing them without replacement leaves about 35 g protein — still
`high-protein`, but the batter also loses its binder and most of its lift.

Claude: to make this properly vegetarian, my suggestion is to raise the cottage cheese
to about 230 g and add a tablespoon of cornflour or a scoop of protein powder for
structure, keeping the baking powder as-is for lift. **This is my reformulation, not
the creator's, and I haven't tested it** — expect a denser pancake than hers.

Claude: the lid at step 5 is doing real work. A high-protein, low-fat batter sets slowly
and scorches before it cooks through unless it steams under a lid.

Claude: don't over-blend at step 3. The creator warns the batter pressurises and erupts
— that's a real thing with baking powder in a sealed blender.
