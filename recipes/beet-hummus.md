## Ingredients

Serves 6–8.

- 1 small-medium beet (~140 g)
- 1 can (425 g) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1/4 tsp baking soda
- Heaping 1/3 cup (90 g) good-quality tahini
- 1 large lemon, zested and juiced (~3 tbsp juice)
- 3 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1/4 tsp ground cumin
- 1/4 tsp ground coriander
- 1 heaping tsp kosher salt
- Freshly cracked black pepper
- 1/2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil (optional)

### To serve
- Extra virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp za'atar (optional)
- Chopped flat-leaf parsley
- Pita chips, flatbread, crackers or crudités

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F. Trim the beet greens, wash and scrub the beet.
2. Rub the beet with olive oil, put it in a baking dish with 1/2 cup water, and
   cover tightly with foil. Roast 45–60 minutes, until fork-tender.
3. Cool slightly, run under water and peel off the skin — wear gloves. Discard the
   stem and roughly chop.
4. Put the drained chickpeas in a saucepan with water and the baking soda. Bring to
   a boil, then simmer 20 minutes until softening.
5. Drain, remove the loose skins, and blend in a food processor for 1–2 minutes to
   a smooth purée.
6. Add the beet, tahini, lemon zest and juice, garlic, cumin, coriander, half the
   salt, and pepper. Blend until smooth.
7. Check the consistency — add 1 tbsp ice water for a looser texture. Taste and
   adjust.
8. Transfer to a bowl, drizzle with olive oil, garnish with za'atar and parsley.

## Source notes

The creator's own tips:

- Canned chickpeas work as well as home-cooked in this recipe, unlike in a classic
  hummus.
- Simmering the chickpeas 15–20 minutes until *really* soft is the key to a smooth
  texture.
- Roasted beets taste best, but store-bought pre-steamed beets work in a pinch.
- You only need one beet — roast a few at once and use the rest in salads.
- Thin leftovers with water and lemon juice, or a wine vinegar, and use as a salad
  dressing.

## Notes

Claude: the baking soda at step 4 is the whole trick. It softens the chickpeas fast
and loosens their skins, and skinless chickpeas are what make hummus silky rather
than grainy. Don't skip it, and do bother removing the floating skins at step 5.

Claude: blending the chickpeas *alone* first (step 5) before anything else goes in
is the other half of the texture. Adding tahini and lemon up front stops them
breaking down properly.

Claude: beets stain badly — gloves at step 3 are genuinely worth it, and so is
a board you don't mind.

Claude: about 6 g protein per serving. It's a dip, not a protein source, despite the
chickpeas.

Claude: the 55 minutes is nearly all unattended beet-roasting. Active work is about
15 minutes, and the beet can be roasted days ahead.
