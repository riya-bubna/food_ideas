## Ingredients

### Filling *(mine — amounts not specified except the miso)*
- Soya granules or chunks, cooked and squeezed very dry
- Zucchini, grated and squeezed very dry
- Carrot, finely grated
- Capsicum, finely chopped
- Spring onion
- Garlic and ginger
- Miso — ~1 tsp for 2 servings *(my amount)*
- Soy sauce — just a little, since the miso is already salty
- Black or white pepper
- Optional: chilli flakes or chilli oil

### Wrappers
- 6–8 rice paper sheets

### Spicy peanut sauce
- 3 tbsp smooth peanut butter
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1.5 tbsp sriracha
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1/4 cup hot water
- Salt, to taste

## Instructions

1. Cook the soya granules or chunks and squeeze them very dry. Grate the zucchini and
   squeeze that dry too. Finely grate the carrot and finely chop the capsicum and
   spring onion.
2. Cook the filling **on high heat** — aromatics first, then the vegetables and soya —
   so the moisture drives off rather than stewing.
3. Season with the miso, a little soy sauce, and black or white pepper. Add chilli
   flakes or chilli oil if you want heat.
4. **Let the filling cool completely before wrapping.** It should be relatively dry,
   or the rice paper goes soggy and tears.
5. Soak the rice paper sheets, fill them, and fold into pockets.
6. Pan-fry until golden and crisp on both sides.
7. Whisk the peanut sauce ingredients together — peanut butter, rice vinegar,
   sriracha, soy sauce, hot water and salt — and serve alongside.

## Notes

Me: cook the veg on high heat and let the mixture cool before wrapping. The filling
has to be relatively dry, otherwise the rice paper gets soggy or tears. That's why
the zucchini and the soya both get squeezed hard.

Claude: the miso is doing what the source's brown sugar did — carrying savouriness
and a little sweetness. Stir it in off the heat or at low heat; boiled miso goes flat
and grainy.

Claude: soak the rice paper one sheet at a time, 3–5 seconds in warm water, and pull
it out while it still feels slightly stiff. It keeps softening on the board. Sheets
soaked to floppy stick to themselves and tear at the fold. Double-wrap if the sheets
are thin — the second layer is what lets them survive the pan.

Claude: soya granules make this comfortably `high-protein` — they're the densest
protein source in the collection, and the peanut sauce adds more. The source's paneer
version would land nearer 18 g a serving.

Claude: no oven anywhere in this, and the air fryer is actively worse here. Rice
paper needs contact with a hot oiled surface to crisp, not moving air.

Claude: the peanut sauce is the same base as the dressing on the
[mango peanut lime salad](mango-peanut-lime-salad.md) — peanut butter, acid, soy,
loosened with liquid. It's also good as a dip for the
[air fryer chilli baby corn](air-fryer-chilli-baby-corn.md), or spooned over the
[veg fried rice](veg-fried-rice.md).

Claude: sriracha at 1.5 tbsp to 3 tbsp peanut butter is properly hot. Start at half
if anyone else is eating.

Claude: your soya-and-miso filling is close to what's already in the
[tempeh veggie burger](tempeh-veggie-burger.md) in spirit — squeezed-dry, seasoned,
bound. If you have filling left over it patties up and pan-fries perfectly well
without any wrapper.
