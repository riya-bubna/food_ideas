**Source:** [Muhammara, Fitgreenmind](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/kGeOmcc0eyo)
— recipe from the creator's pinned comment

The dip in its own right. Compare
[Muhammara Pasta](muhammara-pasta.md), which builds a pasta sauce on the same idea.

## Ingredients

6 servings. Cooking for 2 — use a third of everything, or make the full batch
and keep it.

- 2 bell peppers
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1/4 cup (60 ml) olive oil
- 1/2 cup (50 g) walnuts
- 1/4 cup (25 g) breadcrumbs
- 1 tbsp pomegranate syrup or lemon juice
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp cumin
- 1/2 tsp paprika

## Instructions

1. Roast the peppers and garlic at 175°C.
2. Remove the skins from the peppers.
3. Blend all the ingredients until smooth.

## Notes

Me: made separately as a dip, usually with sweet potato air fries.

Claude: the source gives no roasting time — only the temperature. Go by look:
roughly 30–35 minutes at 175°C, until the skins blister and darken. That's my
estimate, not the creator's.

Claude: steaming the peppers in a covered bowl for 10 minutes straight out of the
oven makes step 2 far easier. The skins slip off instead of tearing.

Claude: this is the walnut-forward ratio — 50 g walnuts to 2 peppers — that the
[muhammara pasta](muhammara-pasta.md) version dilutes. If you liked the pasta
sauce but found it more smoky-pepper than nutty, this is why.

Claude: pomegranate syrup and lemon juice are offered as equivalents but aren't.
The syrup gives the sweet-sour depth that defines muhammara; lemon alone is just
sharp. Use the syrup if you have it.

Claude: keeps about 5 days refrigerated and freezes well, so the full 6-serving
batch is worth making even cooking for two.
