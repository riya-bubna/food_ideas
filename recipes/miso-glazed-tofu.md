**Source:** [Miso Tofu Recipe — Allie Petersen, Naturallie Plant Based](https://naturallieplantbased.com/miso-glazed-tofu/)

Written for 2 — no scaling needed.

## Ingredients

- 16 oz (454 g) block extra firm tofu, drained and pressed
- 2 tbsp white miso paste
- 2 tbsp maple syrup (adjust sweetness to preference)
- 1 tbsp soy sauce or tamari (gluten-free option)
- 1 tsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp ginger paste
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp water

To finish: sesame seeds, spring onions, extra glaze.

## Instructions

1. Whisk together the miso paste, maple syrup, soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil,
   ginger paste, garlic and water until smooth. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
2. Press the tofu and cut it into whatever shape you want — cubes, triangles or
   slices. Put it in a sealable bag with **half** the glaze and toss gently to coat.
   Marinate 15–20 minutes minimum, or up to several hours for deeper flavour.
3. Heat a little sesame oil in a non-stick skillet over medium-high. Add the
   marinated tofu and cook 5–6 minutes a side, until a golden-brown crust forms.
   Add the remaining glaze while it cooks.
4. Take it off the heat when it's as crisp as you want it. Serve hot, garnished with
   sesame seeds and spring onions, with extra glaze over the top. Pair with rice
   and/or vegetables.

### Air fryer option *(the source's own)*

Shake off the excess marinade. Spread the tofu evenly in the basket. Air fry at
400°F (200°C) for 12–15 minutes, shaking halfway, until crisp and golden.

## Source notes

From the creator:

- Marinating overnight deepens the flavour considerably.
- Use your largest skillet, spread the tofu in a single layer, and leave it alone for
  the full 5–6 minutes without flipping — that's what gives the crispiest result.
- Start at medium-high to sear, then drop to medium or medium-low so the glaze
  doesn't burn.
- Keeps 3 days refrigerated in an airtight container. Reheat in the air fryer,
  on the stovetop, or in the microwave.

## Notes

Claude: **the air fryer method here is the source's own, not a conversion** — no oven
anywhere in this recipe, and nothing needs adapting. 200°C for 12–15 minutes, single
layer, shake halfway. A 454 g block will need two batches in a small basket; say so
before you start, because it doubles the cooking window.

Claude: **hold back some glaze for after the air fryer.** Miso and maple burn — the
creator's own warning about dropping the pan heat applies double to a 200°C basket.
Air fry the shaken-off tofu plain-ish, then toss it in the reserved glaze while it's
still hot. That's why the recipe splits the glaze in two in the first place.

Claude: 454 g of extra firm tofu across 2 servings is around 35 g protein each —
this is one of the highest-protein things in the collection, above the
[sticky lemon garlic tofu](sticky-lemon-garlic-tofu.md). Tofu here usually comes in
200 g blocks, so that's a little over two.

Claude: press the tofu properly — 20–30 minutes under something heavy. A watery block
won't take the marinade and won't crust, and this glaze is thin enough that it needs
a dry surface to cling to.

Claude: this is the **protein the [sesame ginger carrot salad](sesame-ginger-carrot-salad.md)
is missing.** That salad is a 4 g-protein side on its own; half this tofu on top of it
makes a complete plate, and the two dressings agree — both are soy, rice vinegar,
sesame oil and ginger, so nothing clashes. Cold sharp ribbons under hot sticky tofu is
the whole appeal.

Claude: your third miso recipe, after the
[miso sweet potato with cornmeal tofu](miso-sweet-potato-cornmeal-tofu.md) and the
filling in the [rice paper dumplings](rice-paper-dumplings-peanut-sauce.md). A tub of
white miso now earns its place in the fridge.

Claude: 2 tbsp maple across 2 servings is a genuinely sweet glaze. The creator flags
this themself — start at 1 to 1.5 tbsp if you want it more savoury, and the miso will
still carry it.
